You may share if ONE of these applies

1Consent

Implied for the team caring for the patient. Ask explicitly for anyone else: police, solicitors, employers.

2Lacks capacity

Share what is in their best interests, for example telling family how they are.

3Required by law

See the red box below. Share only what the law asks for.

4Public interest

To protect others from death or serious harm, or to prevent or detect serious crime. Ask a senior.

None of these? Do not share.

You must share: the law says so

  • New from 30 Sept 2026: information relevant to a child's safety or welfare must be shared with social care or police through the safeguarding team. Consent is not needed. (Children Act 2004, s16LA)
  • FGM in a girl under 18 (she tells you or you see it): you call the police on 101
  • Notifiable disease: tell UKHSA on suspicion. Urgent ones by phone within 24 hours
  • Terrorism: tell the police anything that may help prevent it
  • Police identifying a driver: give identity only, no clinical details
  • Court order or coroner: share only what is required

Common situations

Gunshot (any cause) or knife assault

Usually tell the police, without the patient's name at first. Giving their identity needs consent, the law or a public interest reason. Accidental or self-inflicted knife wounds: don't usually report.

Police ask about a patient

No general duty to answer. You need consent, a legal requirement, a court order or a public interest reason. Involve the consultant in charge.

Relative on the phone

Do not confirm the patient is here without their consent. You can always listen to relatives' concerns.

Worried about a child

Share through the safeguarding team. Usually tell the family, unless that puts the child at risk. Think about children at home of adult patients too.

Adult discloses domestic abuse

If they have capacity and refuse a referral, usually respect it. Share if others, such as children, are at risk of serious harm.

Unfit to drive, won't stop

The patient must tell DVLA. If they keep driving and others are at risk, you should tell DVLA promptly without consent. Tell them first, then in writing.

If you share

  1. Share the minimum needed
  2. Check who you are talking to
  3. Tell the patient, unless unsafe or it would defeat the purpose
  4. Document: what, to whom, when, the legal basis and why

Not sure? Ask first

You must be able to justify your decision. You do not have to make it alone.

  • Consultant in charge
  • Safeguarding team
  • Caldicott Guardian
  • Defence organisation
